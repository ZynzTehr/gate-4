# Proposal for Reyna's Ride Repair

Prepared for Reyna, owner of Reyna's Ride Repair
Prepared by Jorge Bucio

---

Reyna, good talking with you at the shop earlier. When you mentioned wiping grease off your hands between repairs just to answer texts hours late, I promised to write down a plain, one-page plan for how to fix it without giving you another piece of software to manage.

## The problem that came up when we talked

When I asked what was giving you the biggest headache at the shop, you put it plainly. You told me, "I just want to stop losing jobs because I didn't get back to someone."

Right now, when you have a bike in the stand and grease on your hands, you cannot stop every ten minutes to check texts or social messages. By the time you wash up and reply a few hours later, a rider with a flat tire or slipping gears has already called another shop.

Then we looked at the paper calendar by the till. Because repair requests come in through your phone, your social page, and your cousin covering the counter three days a week, neither of you can see the full schedule in one place. That is how two Saturdays got double-booked last month, and how you lost a customer who had already waited a week.

You also mentioned that three different people told you to "get AI to do that," right after you had already wasted money on software you never ended up using. You do not need another app to log into, and you do not need to care what AI means.

## What I will set up for your shop

I spent eleven years working around truck shops, mechanics, and dispatch counters. Nothing I set up will ask you or your cousin to sit at a computer. Everything runs quietly behind the same shop phone number and social page you already use.

**Every customer gets a reply in under a minute.** When someone texts the shop or messages your social page, they immediately get a friendly reply from the shop. It lets them know you are working on a bike and asks what needs fixing and what day they want to drop it off.

**Junk messages get ignored.** Spam texts and sales messages are blocked automatically so your phone only buzzes for real riders.

**One shared schedule stops Saturday double-bookings.** You tell me the maximum number of bikes you want to take on a Saturday or a weekday. Before the system offers a drop-off day to a customer, it checks how many spots are left on one shared phone calendar. Once a Saturday hits your limit, it offers the next open day instead.

**Your cousin keeps the counter simple.** When a walk-in customer books at the till, your cousin taps one link on the counter phone to fill a slot in fifteen seconds. If you still want paper by the register, the system will send a clean daily list every morning that you can print and clip right by the till.

## Why this is worth doing

If replying in under a minute saves you two lost repair jobs a week at $60 to $90 a ticket, that puts $500 to $700 a month back into the shop. It also saves you four or five hours a week of answering texts late at night, and it guarantees you never have to tell a Saturday customer who waited all week that you do not have room for their bike.

## What it costs

It's $650 to build, and about $25 a month to run.

The $650 is a one-time flat price, split into $325 to start and $325 only after it is working on your phone and I have walked you and your cousin through it at the counter.

## The catch

There are two honest limits you should know upfront. First, the system cannot read your paper calendar, so when a walk-in customer books at the register, you or your cousin have to take fifteen seconds to tap the counter link so the system knows that Saturday spot is gone. Second, the system will never guess prices for custom builds or unusual repairs. When a message like that comes in, it holds the customer's spot and texts you a three-line summary so you can price it yourself when your hands are clean.

Take a look at this page tonight. When we talk tomorrow, you can tell me how many bikes you want as your Saturday limit, and if you want to move forward, I can stop by the shop on Monday for twenty minutes to get it started.
